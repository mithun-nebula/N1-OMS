System flow — branching

Organization A — the whole application, one chart

Seven ways an operation can start, spread across the top. They all squeeze through one gate. Below it the chart opens out into the four kinds of work the organization actually does. Then it squeezes again — everything is recorded and published — and opens out a final time into what the change sets off and how it reaches you.
How to read it: across = different kinds of thing · down = the order they happen in · a diamond = a question with two answers · the two narrow points are where nothing can get past. Click any capability in the middle band to see its own decisions.

Why it narrows twice

The two waists are the whole argument. Nothing reaches a record without passing the gate, and nothing changes without being recorded and published. Because the shape pinches there, you can see that before reading a word — and it is why permission and the activity log only have to be built once.

Why four lanes, not twenty-three

The four families are the build scope’s own section headings — the employee side, course work, the workplace, and the foundation items. Twenty-three columns would be unreadable; four columns with the detail one click away keeps the whole map visible at once.

Where the branches actually matter

A form skips the speech and read-back questions. An unattended rule skips them too but answers every question in the gate. Only a spoken request ever reaches “is the room shared?” at the bottom. Straight down, those three journeys looked identical. Here they don’t.

31 of the build scope’s 32 capabilities are on this page — 9 on the spine, 22 in the four lanes. Nothing names a language, a framework or a provider: only what happens, and on what condition.
